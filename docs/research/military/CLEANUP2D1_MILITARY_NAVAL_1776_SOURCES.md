# CLEANUP-2D-1 — Military & Naval 1776 Sources

## Source policy

This bibliography records the sources actually used in the research matrix. Major powers use primary/official/academic material wherever available. For low-evidence polities, regional scholarly synthesis is used only to define military structure; it is **not** used to fabricate a national headcount. Near-date evidence outside 1775–1777 is explicitly marked as lower-confidence in the master CSVs.

## Great Britain / Atlantic Revolution

### British Army | Oxford Bibliographies in Atlantic History
- **Author / institution:** Oxford University Press
- **Type:** Scholarly reference overview
- **URL:** https://academic.oup.com/reference/62333/reference-article-abstract/554063602
- **Use / limitation:** British Army peacetime establishment reached almost 50,000 by 1775.

### American War of Independence: Outbreak
- **Author / institution:** National Army Museum
- **Type:** National military museum
- **URL:** https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/american-war-independence-outbreak
- **Use / limitation:** About 7,000 British regulars in North America in 1775; 11 battalions at Boston, one at New York, six elsewhere.

### The New York Campaign, 1776
- **Author / institution:** U.S. Army Center of Military History
- **Type:** Official military history
- **URL:** https://history.army.mil/Publications/Publications-Catalog/The-New-York-Campaign/
- **Use / limitation:** British 1776 New York plan envisaged an army of about 30,000; wartime expedition, not global peace establishment.

### American Revolution
- **Author / institution:** Naval History and Heritage Command
- **Type:** Official naval history
- **URL:** https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/browse-by-topic/wars-conflicts-and-operations/american-revolution.html
- **Use / limitation:** Royal Navy about 270 warships in 1776; colonies about 27 warships.

### Washington takes command of Continental Army in 1775
- **Author / institution:** U.S. Army
- **Type:** Official military history
- **URL:** https://www.army.mil/article/40819/washington_takes_command_of_continental_army_in_1775
- **Use / limitation:** 1776 reorganization plan: 20,372 men uniformly paid/supplied/administered by Congress, enlisted through end of 1776.

### History of the Medical Department in the Revolutionary War – Boston/New York transition
- **Author / institution:** U.S. Army Medical Department Center of History & Heritage
- **Type:** Official military history
- **URL:** https://achh.army.mil/history/book-rev-medmen-medmench03/
- **Use / limitation:** Army strength 18,410 officers and men on 9 March 1776; useful effective-strength check.

### US Navy Personnel Strength, 1775 to Present
- **Author / institution:** Naval History and Heritage Command
- **Type:** Official naval historical statistics
- **URL:** https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/u/usn-personnel-strength.html
- **Use / limitation:** 1776: 90 officers + 3,000 enlisted = 3,090.

### Uniforms of the U.S. Navy 1776–1783
- **Author / institution:** Naval History and Heritage Command
- **Type:** Official naval history
- **URL:** https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/heritage/uniforms-and-personal-equipment/uniforms-1776-1783.html
- **Use / limitation:** Four ships procured in Oct 1775; thirteen frigates authorized Dec 1775; authorization is not the same as ships active on 1 Jan 1776.

### Continental Congress and the Navy
- **Author / institution:** Naval History and Heritage Command
- **Type:** Official naval chronology
- **URL:** https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/c/continental-congress-and-the-navy.html
- **Use / limitation:** First 74-gun line-of-battle ship was only authorized on 20 Nov 1776.

### Karlskrona shipyard – the greatest investment of Sweden's era as a great power

### Fredric Henric af Chapman
- **Author / institution:** Svenskt Biografiskt Lexikon / Riksarkivet
- **Type:** National archival/biographical reference
- **URL:** https://sok.riksarkivet.se/sbl/mobil/Artikel/14787
- **Use / limitation:** At end of 1776 Chapman was tasked with a full renewal plan for the line fleet, corroborating transition/obsolescence.

### Naval Seaman, A Poor Man's Job
- **Author / institution:** Jaap R. Bruijn, The Dutch Navy of the Seventeenth and Eighteenth Centuries / Cambridge
- **Type:** Academic monograph chapter
- **URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/dutch-navy-of-the-seventeenth-and-eighteenth-centuries/naval-seaman-a-poor-mans-job/9C70FB2D51C7815EFE66B881B28BA996
- **Use / limitation:** After 1713, typical peacetime Dutch naval activity used only a few thousand seamen annually; Amsterdam Admiralty handled ~3/4 of vessels, mainly lower-rated SOL/frigates.

## South Asia / companies

### Recruitment and Reform in the East India Company Army, 1760–1800
- **Author / institution:** Arthur N. Gilbert, Journal of British Studies / Cambridge
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed article
- **URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-british-studies/article/abs/recruitment-and-reform-in-the-east-india-company-army-17601800/917E89E800A905219FA8881EBCBF055F
- **Use / limitation:** Coromandel return: 2,502 Europeans + 15,490 sepoys (near-1760s evidence); 1778: 10,926 Europeans + 70,093 natives serving the armies. Not safe to linearly interpolate to 1776.

### The hybrid military establishment of the East India Company in South Asia: 1750–1849
- **Author / institution:** Kaushik Roy, Journal of Global History / Cambridge
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed article
- **URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-global-history/article/abs/hybrid-military-establishment-of-the-east-india-company-in-south-asia-17501849/5231F27DF10E4755D017A92704C08672
- **Use / limitation:** EIC power relied on a hybrid British/South Asian military establishment; manpower, animals and logistics were South Asian as well as European.

### The military revolution in South Asia, 1750–1850
- **Author / institution:** Peter A. Lorge, Cambridge University Press
- **Type:** Academic book chapter
- **URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/asian-military-revolution/military-revolution-in-south-asia-17501850/4CE7B83571AA90662C44497252E8F42A
- **Use / limitation:** European-trained infantry and improved artillery spread after 1750, while cavalry remained important.

### Warriors and States: Military labour in southern India, circa 1750–1800
- **Author / institution:** Mesrob Vartavarian, Modern Asian Studies / Cambridge
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed article
- **URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/modern-asian-studies/article/abs/warriors-and-states-military-labour-in-southern-india-circa-17501800/6D1E15184371BC18D75954F0E2E7A865
- **Use / limitation:** Many South Indian states relied on mercenaries, warrior groups and mobilized manpower alongside weak/variable standing establishments.

### The Marathas 1600–1818
- **Author / institution:** Stewart Gordon, Cambridge University Press
- **Type:** Academic monograph
- **URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/marathas-16001818/1098497E96D2AFA760D18DB311A91C80/listing
- **Use / limitation:** Maratha warfare relied heavily on cavalry, large logistical trains, artillery, infantry and a camp bazaar; headcounts cannot be mapped as a simple European standing army.

### Haidar ‘Ali and Tipu Sultan: Mysore's Eighteenth-century Rulers in Transition
- **Author / institution:** Kaveh Yazdani, Itinerario / Cambridge
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed article
- **URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/itinerario/article/abs/haidar-ali-and-tipu-sultan-mysores-eighteenthcentury-rulers-in-transition/FB1291A1D21FB308D7819FC034CA3C6F

### Labouring Transformations of Amphibious Monsters: the VOC, 1600–1800
- **Author / institution:** International Review of Social History / Cambridge
- **Type:** Peer-reviewed article
- **URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-review-of-social-history/article/labouring-transformations-of-amphibious-monsters-exploring-early-modern-globalization-diversity-and-shifting-clusters-of-labour-relations-in-the-context-of-the-dutch-east-india-company-16001800/6D2A9DF44C70C44B1AA6BFB773AFF7E2
- **Use / limitation:** VOC combined trade, warfare and government; roughly a quarter to a third of the workforce were military personnel; cannot be mapped to a sovereign national army/navy without company-specific reconstruction.
