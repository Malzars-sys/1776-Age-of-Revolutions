[h1]Update 2.6 — An expanded 1776 world, now compatible with Victoria 3 1.15[/h1]

This update brings together the Victoria 3 1.15 / State and Revolution compatibility work, a major territorial rework, and new military, diplomatic and economic options.

[b]Restart the game and begin a new campaign to receive the revised borders, populations, laws, buildings and starting armies. Existing saves will not automatically receive these changes.[/b]

[h2]Compatibility and stability[/h2]
[list]
[*]Adapted scripts, laws, events, objectives and journal entries to version 1.15 while retaining the 1 January 1776 start.
[*]Fixed the crash caused by naval AI ship-category allocation when unpausing.
[*]Updated war support, war goals, infamy and strait-related treaty articles.
[*]Restored content masked by older definitions, including national law variants, succession events, coups, agitators and Japanese event chains.
[*]Corrected references to removed cultures, missing modifiers and events, and obsolete triggers.
[/list]

[h2]The map and political world of 1776[/h2]
[list]
[*]Continued the Holy Roman Empire rework, adding and revising imperial states, principalities, ecclesiastical territories, cities and interwoven possessions.
[*]Revised the Balkans and Ottoman–Venetian interfaces, including Ragusa, Montenegro, Cattaro, the Ionian possessions, Albania, Epirus and Greece.
[*]Revised Scandinavia and Finland, Russia's borders, the Caucasus, Central Asia, and several interfaces in the Middle East and Asia.
[*]Extensive African revisions covering the Maghreb and Egypt, Senegambia and the Sahel, Hausa states, the Chad basin, Nile valley, Horn of Africa, Congo, Great Lakes, Mozambique, Madagascar and southern Africa.
[*]Revised colonial and Indigenous territories in the Americas, with additional polities and reworked regional borders.
[*]Adjusted populations, cultures, capitals, governments, rulers and subject relationships to the revised territorial setup. Corrected inconsistent tributary links and impassable-province problems.
[/list]

[h2]The Holy Roman Empire and diplomacy[/h2]
[list]
[*]Added a more developed imperial framework: imperial estates and electors, elections, constitutional arrangements, cooperation principles, reforms and centralization.
[*]Added interactions for imperial obligations, territorial restitution, secession and autonomy, alongside interface tools for reviewing the imperial situation.
[*]Added forms of protection and autonomy suited to unrecognized powers and selected regional relationships.
[/list]

[h2]The American Revolution[/h2]
[list]
[*]Reworked the opening of the aid chain. France first decides whether to contact the insurgents; Congress can then request assistance and negotiate its commitments, including when the United States is AI-controlled.
[*]Added a ten-year Franco-American convention providing £30,000 per week, 15 small arms, one artillery and military assistance in exchange for an obligation and debt recognition.
[*]Added interest-free and 5% interest offers on the planned principal, deferred repayment and diplomatic consequences for repudiation. Fixed failed ratification and text that did not distinguish the two offers.
[*]French intervention becomes progressively more likely. Spain starts outside the war and may be called by France through the Family Compact. Foreign intervention in this war now requires an appropriate regional or diplomatic connection.
[*]Minutemen companies are active from the start and replace their parent militia variant in the American law list. Victory can open a militia reform process; defeat retains the return to Peasant Levies.
[*]Revised American forces and commanders, moved Britain's West Indies garrison to Guatemala, and reduced the insurgents' territorial losses after defensive defeats.
[*]The White House can now be constructed in the District of Columbia after researching Liberal Constitutionalism.
[/list]

[h2]Land fortifications and engineer corps[/h2]
[list]
[*]Added land forts in inland states, capped at five levels, with three garrison methods following the naval fortification technology progression.
[*]Forts improve local defense and reduce casualties and territorial losses. At full staffing, five levels reduce territory lost by 25%, 50% or 75%, depending on the selected method.
[*]Added increasing effective construction costs of approximately 900, 1,100, 1,300, 1,500 and 1,700 points before other bonuses. The displayed cost remains 900; extensions progress more slowly.
[*]Added the engineer corps, unlocked by its namesake technology and specialized in dismantling fort protection. At full strength and participating in the battle, 25, 50 or 75 battalions neutralize the corresponding five-level fort defenses.
[*]Added cultural engineer illustrations and direct numerical fort-protection and counter-fort readouts in tooltips.
[/list]

[h2]Naval changes[/h2]
[list]
[*]Armed merchantmen now have their own silhouette and three equipment tiers, bronze, silver and gold, unlocked over time. Their characteristics emphasize transport and escort duties.
[*]Added an armed-merchantman role modification and corrected traditional shipbuilding and hull-sheathing icons.
[*]Added starting ports in British Guatemala and the Mosquito Kingdom's Nicaragua to restore maritime-link options.
[/list]

[h2]Economy, infrastructure and technology[/h2]
[list]
[*]Added Hardware as a distinct good, produced through a secondary furniture-manufactory group and providing new demand for alloys and metals.
[*]Rebalanced metallurgical recipes and copper, resolving collisions between mod and 1.15 definitions.
[*]Extended railway transport production methods to the relevant industries and agricultural buildings, without adding a competing group to urban centers or government buildings.
[*]Extended Scientific Management to urban industries. Increased road transport output; turnpike roads now consume wood without tools.
[*]Expanded the Anzin Mining Company's activities and added its prestige coal. Revised several industrial locations, paper mills, logging camps and livestock farms.
[*]Rebalanced Britain's starting infrastructure, tool-workshop distribution, glassworks, ceramics, chemicals and agricultural methods. Removed added tool consumption from subsistence farms and cleaned up duplicate production methods.
[*]Restored Centralization's vanilla name and internal identifier. Adjusted construction-sector, ordinary-trade and alloy-processing prerequisites.
[/list]

[h2]Laws, events and interface[/h2]
[list]
[*]Added a Prussian military amendment: −15% military goods costs and military wages, balanced by −10% military research speed.
[*]Revised Russia's starting administration with Military Bureaucrats, Noble Legacy and Caesaropapism, and restored national law variants available later.
[*]Enabled Russian Internal Colonization and the Cossacks amendment, with four starting settlements and decentralized northern territories open to colonization.
[*]Japanese famine events now offer meaningful economic and political choices: building reserves, village relief, monitoring, requisitions and grain distribution.
[*]Fixed Morocco's three regional anarchy bars and their penalty calculations, including monthly updates and save/reload behavior.
[*]Reorganized journal groups, including Napoleonic Wars and Ottoman Decline, and corrected Sick Man of Europe visibility.
[*]Adapted the supply tutorial to merchant marine. Fixed Acre journal previews, several missing tooltips and the French grain label incorrectly displaying artillery.
[*]Added icons, flags and illustrations, with numerous French and English localization corrections.
[/list]

The Workshop package contains the data and assets required to play. Development tools, research and reports are excluded.

The territorial rework continues. Further border refinement and expanded national content are planned for future updates.
