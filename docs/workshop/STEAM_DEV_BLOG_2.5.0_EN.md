# Update 2.5.0 — Imperial Reform, Naval Balance and Visual Polish

Hello everyone!

Before moving into the next stage of regional map research and rework, this update brings together a substantial round of improvements to the 1776 start. The focus is on imperial politics, starting economies, military progression and a more consistent visual presentation.

## Two Ottoman crises, two different challenges

The Ottoman Empire now receives two separate opening events, each introducing its own Journal Entry. Their descriptions and narrative excerpts are written to fit the setting, while their tooltips explain the consequences of accepting the single opening choice.

**The Burden of the Sublime Porte** concerns administration, taxation and institutions. Its ten independent objectives progressively relieve the initial administrative burden: each fulfilled objective removes ten percentage points of additional tax waste and offsets one percentage point of the bureaucracy penalty. Progress is recalculated weekly, so losing an objective also means losing its relief.

Home Affairs, Police and Education must each reach level 3 for their respective objectives. All ten objectives must remain fulfilled together for twelve consecutive months to complete the journal. The interface now displays the counters correctly and distinguishes the current, temporary relief from the effects of final completion.

**An Empire of Many Masters** concerns provincial authority, subjects and political stability. Centralisation, a negotiated settlement and the restoration of state authority remain alternative paths. Resolving this crisis also requires Rifling, Abolitionism and an end to the slave-trade law, including its variants. These requirements belong to the political journal, not the ten administrative objectives.

The political crisis increases subjects' weekly liberty desire and makes foreign support for separatism easier. Its deadline is 1 January 1836, although severe, prolonged instability can cause an earlier failure and lead into **The Sick Man of Europe**. Administrative reform remains independent, and the 1776 setup avoids reapplying the legacy administrative penalty through that transition.

The Ottoman starting army has also been raised to **72 regular battalions**, excluding conscripts. Ten directly Ottoman government-administration levels have been removed across Constantinople, Ankara and Syria; Egyptian and other subject administrations are not included in that reduction.

## Diplomacy without automatic historical outcomes

The starting diplomatic setup has been revised, including France's Ottoman trade privileges, the Franco-Spanish Family Compact, the Franco-Austrian defensive pact and the Anglo-Portuguese alliance. Premature French and Spanish independence-support agreements with the American rebels have been removed from the January bookmark.

Egypt is represented as an Ottoman subject with its own Mamluk leadership, army and transferred local buildings. Hanover's personal union with Britain has also been adjusted.

Poland-Lithuania and Crimea begin within the Russian power bloc, without being automatically annexed. An old timed Polish partition script has been disabled: simply waiting out its timer will no longer hand territory to neighbouring powers. This does not yet introduce a complete replacement partition system.

## Fleets that are more affordable to maintain

Caravels, galleys and cogs expand the mod's early naval options. These classes trade combat strength for substantially lower construction and supply requirements. Starting fleets now use a broader mixture of economical hulls and advanced ships, with technologically developed naval powers retaining some frigates and ships of the line.

Britain's fleet has been reduced from 120 to **97 ships**, including a Channel Fleet of 40 instead of 50. Naval establishments and crew requirements have been adjusted, including corrections to crew-slot rounding and the location of British recruitment capacity.

The new hulls have dedicated silhouettes, names and ship-designer equipment icons. Their equipment follows the existing naval technology tree; the 3D representations currently reuse standard wooden-ship models. Frigates unlock through Naval Architecture, ships of the line through Marine Chronometry, and whaling stations again require Marine Chronometry.

## Starting armies and overseas deployments

Several European starting armies have been brought into line with their researched military technologies. France now starts with **four field-artillery battalions per army corps**, replacing the previous two bombard battalions.

Bombards now consume **0.5 iron per battalion** instead of artillery goods, avoiding an input requirement that could precede artillery production. Musket infantry unlocks through Light Infantry Tactics; line infantry follows through Armament Standards.

British North American and Mediterranean deployments, Canadian garrisons and Atlantic fleet positioning have also received adjustments. These changes improve the starting setup without promising that every assigned conscript will already be recruited on the first day.

## Economic and infrastructure adjustments

Britain's starting administration, industry, mining and agriculture have been rebalanced. Mine production methods have been updated, some surplus production capacity has been reduced, and part of the rye-farming setup has been replaced with livestock and wool production. Additional cotton plantations support the American colonial economy.

British India's government-administration capacity has been expanded, with supporting infrastructure and British ownership assigned to declared private buildings where appropriate. Public administrations remain locally state-owned. To address recruitment restrictions, part of the starting population in selected Company states has been reassigned to accepted cultures while preserving regional population totals. This is a gameplay workaround, not a historical demographic claim.

Other regional changes include additional livestock production in France and the German states, more silk and cotton in Provence, extra tooling capacity in Île-de-France and Wallonia, and adjustments to Wallonian logging and iron production. Piedmont's shared logging potential has been increased, improving Genoa's available forestry capacity as well as Sardinia's.

Traditional dirt roads no longer consume wood: their operating expense comes from employment. Higher road-production methods now provide small amounts of Transportation alongside Infrastructure. Selected starting road networks have been upgraded and resized, and canal bonuses have been adjusted.

AI countries now strongly favour the best available regional-infrastructure methods. A startup and monthly check updates existing road and railway networks, while canals are concentrated in each country's ten highest-GDP states. This controller does not operate on player countries and does not grant technologies or create buildings.

Scientific Management now consumes Organised Research Data and replaces its flat throughput bonus with a smaller, level-scaled bonus. Diesel-pump output for iron and lead has increased from **70 to 85**. Colonial Slavery now unlocks through Colonisation, and Arc Welding has been moved to a later era.

## Portraits and a clearer visual language

Frederick the Great receives a revised portrait with a powdered wig, fitted hat, decorative trim, black ribbon and cane. Selected historical monarchs also receive the powdered-wig accessory.

A broad icon pass replaces many missing or unsuitable production-method images, fixes Precision Machinery's small goods icon and harmonises production-method group colours. Locomotives use green without recolouring wagon icons; medical services use teal, military and canal groups red, secondary production purple, and standard production yellow.

New artwork covers industrial processes, automation, healthcare and canal methods. Existing suitable vanilla and TKR assets have been retained where possible. The chemical-plant building icon has also been revised to better match the game's elevated-view composition. Copper's individual production-method placeholders remain outside this completed pass.

## Before starting a campaign

**Restart the game and start a new campaign to receive the revised buildings, populations, formations and diplomatic setup.** Existing saves will not automatically acquire all changes to starting history.

The Ottoman and infrastructure scripts have passed static and logic checks, and the lightweight Workshop package has been verified against the development files. Exact budget balances, recruitment and long-term AI behaviour still need feedback from real campaigns; this is not a claim that every remaining issue is resolved.

The upcoming regional map rework is **not included** in this update. Regional research will guide that next stage rather than being presented as finished content here.

Thank you for the reports, screenshots and suggestions. If you encounter a problem, please include the country, in-game date, whether it is a new campaign, and your active mod list. Budget and bureaucracy screenshots are especially useful for the next balancing pass!
